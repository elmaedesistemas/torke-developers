TorkeKit's automated tests simulate runs with exact physics (so the real time is known) and feed them through the engine with the worst of a phone: **1 Hz GPS, sensor noise and a tilted mount**. Every release must meet these targets:

| Mark | Target |
| --- | --- |
| 60 ft | ± 0.10 s |
| 0–100 km/h | ± 0.20 s |
| 1/4 mile | ± 0.15 s |

On top of that, iOS and Android must give **the same result** from the same samples: the tests on both platforms read `demo-42.torkerun.json` and compare the times to half a millisecond.

## What helps and what hurts

| Helps | Hurts |
| --- | --- |
| Rigid mount | Phone in the hand or loose |
| Open sky | Roofs, trees, buildings |
| GPS at 5 Hz or faster | 1 Hz GPS with dropped fixes |
| Flat strip | Slope (gravity leaks in as acceleration) |
| One second still before launching | Rolling off the line |

## The phone's GPS

TorkeKit works with the phone alone, without external receivers or other hardware. To get the most from the GPS it has:

- **The real rate.** `snapshot.gpsRate` says how many fixes per second each phone's GPS gives: 1 on iPhone and most Android phones, 5 to 10 on some. The engine uses every fix that arrives.
- **Full tracking on Android.** From Android 12, `PhoneSensorProvider` asks the GNSS chip to track every satellite without power-saving gaps while recording. Fixes come steadier; it uses a bit more battery. `PhoneSensorProvider(context, fullTracking = false)` turns it off.
- **Satellites and signal on Android.** `phone.gnss` says how many satellites the GPS uses, how many it sees and their mean signal (C/N0). Under open sky a phone uses 15 or more at 30–40 dB-Hz; with fewer, the speed is less accurate.
- **Why not raw Doppler.** The chip already computes the speed from the satellites' Doppler shift; recomputing it by hand needs each satellite's ephemeris and doesn't improve it.

## Check it with your runs

The targets above are checked with simulated runs. With real runs and their timeslips, [`torke-validate`](/en/guias/validacion/) tells you how far off TorkeKit is at each mark.

<Aside type="tip" title="Got timeslips?">
If you time runs at the track with TorkeKit, keep the recording (`.torkerun.json`) with a photo of the timeslip and run them through `torke-validate`. If you want to share them to improve the engine, open an issue in the [repository](https://github.com/elmaedesistemas/torke-kit-ios/issues).
</Aside>
