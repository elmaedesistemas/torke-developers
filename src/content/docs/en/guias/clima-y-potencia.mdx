The same car is slower on a hot, humid day or at a high-altitude track: the air is thinner and the engine makes less power. To compare runs from different days, TorkeKit **corrects the times to standard air** (25 °C, 990 hPa of dry air, the SAE J1349 standard).

## Try it

<WeatherLab />

Move the weather and watch the demo run change. At 31 °C, 70 % humidity and 910 hPa (an afternoon at a track about 900 m up) the air behaves as if you were at almost 1,800 m, and the car loses 14 % of its power: the demo run would drop from 12.826 to 12.196 s in standard air.

## In code

<Tabs syncKey="platform">
<TabItem label="Swift">

```swift
let weather = Weather(temperatureC: 31, humidity: 70, pressureHpa: 910)

weather.densityAltitude          // meters: higher = thinner air
weather.airDensity               // kg/m³
weather.correctionFactor         // above 1: the air cost power

let corrected = result.corrected(for: weather)
corrected.quarterMile            // the 1/4 in standard air
```

</TabItem>
<TabItem label="Kotlin">

```kotlin
val weather = Weather(temperatureC = 31.0, humidity = 70.0, pressureHpa = 910.0)

weather.densityAltitude          // meters: higher = thinner air
weather.airDensity               // kg/m³
weather.correctionFactor         // above 1: the air cost power

val corrected = result.corrected(weather)
corrected.quarterMile            // the 1/4 in standard air
```

</TabItem>
</Tabs>

<Aside type="caution" title="Station pressure, not the forecast's">
`pressureHpa` is the **actual pressure at the track**. Weather apps usually show sea-level pressure, which at a high track is very different. Use a barometer, the phone's (`CMAltimeter` on iOS, the `TYPE_PRESSURE` sensor on Android) or a nearby weather station.
</Aside>

Times are corrected with the cube root of power (`time × factor^-1/3`) and speeds the other way (`speed × factor^1/3`). The trace stays as measured.

## Estimated power

With the car's weight **including the driver** and the quarter-mile trap speed, Hale's formula gives a good estimate of flywheel horsepower:

<Tabs syncKey="platform">
<TabItem label="Swift">

```swift
// Trap speed: the most reliable
PowerEstimate.fromTrapSpeed(massKg: 1350, quarterMileKmh: result.quarterMileKmh!)

// From the ET: reads low if the car spins at the launch
PowerEstimate.fromElapsedTime(massKg: 1350, quarterMile: result.quarterMile!)

// Wheel power along the run
let curve = PowerEstimate.wheelPower(result, massKg: 1350, dragArea: 0.6)
let peak = PowerEstimate.peakWheelHp(result, massKg: 1350)
```

</TabItem>
<TabItem label="Kotlin">

```kotlin
// Trap speed: the most reliable
PowerEstimate.fromTrapSpeed(massKg = 1350.0, quarterMileKmh = result.quarterMileKmh!!)

// From the ET: reads low if the car spins at the launch
PowerEstimate.fromElapsedTime(massKg = 1350.0, quarterMile = result.quarterMile!!)

// Wheel power along the run
val curve = PowerEstimate.wheelPower(result, massKg = 1350.0, dragArea = 0.6)
val peak = PowerEstimate.peakWheelHp(result, massKg = 1350.0)
```

</TabItem>
</Tabs>

`wheelPower` adds what it took to accelerate the car, beat the air and the tires' rolling resistance. The parameters:

| Parameter | Default | Typical values |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `dragArea` | 0.7 m² | Drag coefficient × frontal area. 0.6 for a sedan, 0.8 for a pickup. |
| `rollingResistance` | 0.015 | 0.012 on drag radials, 0.015 on street tires. |
| `airDensity` | 1.2 kg/m³ | `weather.airDensity` if you have it. |

<Aside type="note">
These are approximations within about 15 %. They're for **comparing changes on the same car** (another turbo, other tires), not for replacing a dyno.
</Aside>
